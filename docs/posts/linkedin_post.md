Can AI tell us who pays for concurrent delay? I think we've been asking it the wrong question.

In my new article I trace 25 years of case law, from Malmaison to Thomas Barnes. I explain why reading SHAP or Shapley values as liability shares goes wrong, and show what explainable AI can do instead: reveal what a delay dispute actually turns on.

Three findings:
→ Only one rule reproduces five English decisions: the employer's delay must actually push completion out, and if it does, concurrent contractor delay doesn't cut the extension.
→ Money converges across the English readings. Time doesn't, and it has a cliff edge.
→ On a real FIDIC 1999 road project, the doctrines differed by 4 days. Site access and a 28-day notice decided the outcome.
