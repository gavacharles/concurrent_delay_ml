Concurrent delay · 1999–2024

How the law on concurrent delay got here

Twenty-five years of English and Scottish decisions, the SCL Protocol and the standard forms. Each entry shows what it decided, which rule it supports, and how carefully it has been checked.

Three jurisdictions on one line

Three phases

1999–2009Time, not money

Malmaison gives the contractor time despite its own concurrent delay. Royal Brompton insists the employer event must actually affect completion. The SCL Protocol's first edition takes a broad view.

2010–2016Actual delay vs apportionment

Scotland apportions where no cause is dominant (City Inn). England refuses (Walter Lilly) and tightens the test: both causes must actually delay completion, felt at the same time (Adyard, Saga).

2017–2024Contracting out, time vs money

The 2017 forms either leave concurrency to the parties (FIDIC 8.5) or price it prospectively (NEC4). Cyden upholds a clause that excludes it. Thomas Barnes separates 119 days of time from 27 days of money.
